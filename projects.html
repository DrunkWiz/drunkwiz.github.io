Lead Time

Submitted to ForgeHacks Online 2026. Solo build.

Food trends usually blow up overseas first. Lead Time watches weekly search interest for 22 foods across seven markets, works out how long each one has historically taken to reach Singapore, and tells a small food business which trends are heading its way. For each food it gives a verdict (Go, Go but late, Wait and watch, or Skip), checks whether the kitchen can actually make it, and then helps with the launch: a suggested price and margin, when to bulk order, a launch offer with its break-even, and a dated posting plan.

It grades its own forecasts

Every calculation takes an "as of" date, and a test makes sure no future data leaks in, so the app can be rewound to any past week and checked against what really happened. The results aren't flattering, and the app shows them anyway. Of 4 arrival forecasts that could be strictly checked, 1 fell inside its window. For Korean yogurt ice cream, the window was 19 weeks too early. When past arrivals are too uneven, it says "Wait and watch" instead of inventing a date.

The model doesn't get the last word

An open model judges what the kitchen can make and drafts the launch copy, but every answer is schema-checked. Anything with dates, links, quantities or popularity claims the data doesn't support is rejected and rewritten. All the numbers come from plain Python, and if every model is down, labelled offline rules take over.

Voucher Kaki

Finalist, SimplifyNext IGNITE Agentic AI Hackathon 2026. Built with a team of four.

Say or type what you feel like eating, in English, 中文, Bahasa Melayu or தமிழ், and Voucher Kaki finds CDC-voucher eateries near you. Results are ranked by relevance and walking distance, with a deliberate nudge toward independent shops over chains. When it has to widen the search to fill the list, it tells you what it gave up instead of quietly showing worse matches.

The best decision was a deletion

We built Google ratings into every result, then took them out. Many of the small shops in our data have no Google listing at all, so a ratings line would put a 4.5 with 800 reviews next to a blank. Someone choosing lunch on a phone picks the number. The whole point of the app is to surface the places people overlook, and a half-empty ratings column would have pushed them straight back to the famous ones.

Recall

2nd place, American Express AI Hackathon 2026.

When a card member doesn't recognise a charge and the merchant shares nothing about it, Recall rebuilds the memory from their own card history: the flight, the hotel, the trip that charge belongs to. It picks up where merchant-supplied details run out, which is most of the long tail of small shops.

The model is never allowed to be sure

The worst thing Recall could do is talk someone out of reporting real fraud. So the model only proposes. Every claim has to cite a real transaction, a second model from a different family checks it, and plain rules code makes every decision that touches money. When the history is too thin, Recall shows the raw details and says nothing at all. Silence is always an acceptable answer.
